Force New Tab

What's new in 2.0.1

More middle-clicks, fewer dead ends

Force New Tab tries to open your destination in a background tab, including many cards and buttons that normally ignore middle-click. It uses the same rules across permitted websites.

Some controls still have limits. A website may have no separate destination, refuse an automatic click or fail to recreate a popup in a copy. As a last resort, finding the destination may change the original page too. Firefox-protected pages remain unsupported.

Found a website that does not work? Please tell us in the extension's Firefox reviews or on GitHub. Include the website, the exact item you middle-clicked and what happened in both tabs.

What's improved

  • Broader opening methods. Links, images and clear card destinations open directly. Harder buttons can use a background copy of the page.
  • Better preparation in copied pages. The extension waits for the matching button to settle before pressing it once. Canva's Login popup has now been confirmed working in the maintainer's test.
  • One simple toggle. All opening methods run automatically. There are no extra feature switches or click-recording tools in this public release.
  • Updated fist artwork. A larger, centered fist and tab replace the earlier design, with clear ON and OFF states.

Middle-click as usual

No Shift key is needed. Click the toolbar fist to turn the whole extension on or off; your choice is remembered.

If the fist is hidden, open Firefox's Extensions menu and pin Force New Tab to the toolbar. Reload pages after first installing or replacing the extension. Ordinary left-clicks remain the way to use the website normally.

Some buttons need a little longer

Known destinations open promptly. When no address can be found, an eligible button can be tried in a background copy instead. Allow up to 15 seconds for that page to load and prepare.

Switching tabs or looking at the copy is fine. Clicking or typing inside it stops the automatic press. A copy reloads the website, so temporary menus, unsaved information or changing feeds may not be identical.

If copying definitely fails before a click is attempted, the final fallback may use the original button to learn its destination. The original page can change as a result. Once a click may have reached the copy, it is never automatically repeated on the original.

Help improve website support

When reporting a problem, include your browser and extension versions, whether sign-in is needed, and whether the new tab showed the right page. Check the original page's appearance as well as its address.

Public links are helpful. For private pages, describe the screen without sharing account numbers, passwords or private messages. There is no automatic report upload or telemetry.
